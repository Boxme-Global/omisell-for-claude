# Omisell: channel comparison

Goal: show which channel earns the most after cancels, returns and fees, and which one is slowest, so the owner knows where to push and where to fix.

Use the last 7 days unless the user gives a period (max 90 days; 30 days gives steadier rates, offer it). Every call below uses the same `date_from`/`date_to` in unix seconds.

## Steps

1. **Sales per store.** `sales_by_channel`: per store `store_name`, `platform`, `count_order`, `gross_sales`, `net_sales`, `return_sales_completed` (refunded on completed customer returns), `cogs`. Ignore `return_sales`: it is the value of failed deliveries, not customer returns. Sum stores of the same platform for the channel row; keep store rows when a platform has several stores and the user cares.
   - Refused (staff): say sales by store needs the shop owner, then call `sales_summary` with `group_by: ["channel"]` (orders and sales per platform, no COGS). Do not use `aggregate_orders` for this: it counts orders but has no sales.
2. **Cancel rate.** `cancel_analysis` with `group_by: ["channel"]`: `by_channel` rows `{key, total, cancelled, cancel_rate}`.
3. **Fees.** `fee_aggregate_by_channel` with `group_by: "channel"` and `fee_codes: ["commission_fee", "transaction_fee", "service_fee", "campaign_fee", "sponsored_affiliates", "infrastructure_fee", "fulfill_fee", "other_fee"]` (without them the total also mixes in shipping and refund lines). Refused → say it needs the order-cost permission and leave the column out.
4. **Speed and failed deliveries.** `fulfillment_lead_time` with `group_by: "store"`: per store `avg_confirm_order`, `avg_packed_order`, `avg_shipped_order`, `avg_delivery_order`, `avg_success_order` (seconds; show hours or days), `count_order`, `count_order_return`. `count_order_return` counts orders whose delivery failed and went back to the shop, NOT customer returns: label it "failed delivery" (share = `count_order_return / count_order`).
5. **Return requests.** `return_pipeline_state` with `group_by: "channel"`: return slips created in the window per platform, in any status (rejected and cancelled requests included), as `rows` of `{key, count}`. Share = slips / that platform's `total` from step 2. Label it "return requests (share of orders)" and say it includes requests not yet accepted.

Rows are keyed by platform code (steps 2, 3, 5) or by store (steps 1, 4). Step 4 rows carry the store name but no platform: map store to platform through step 1's rows; for staff (no step 1 rows) show speed per store name in a second small table. Show the platform's display name (Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop...), not the code.

## Answer

- Summary: the channel with the most sales, the one with the best result after fees and returns, and the weakest point found (high cancel rate, high fees or slow delivery).
- Table: channel · orders · net sales (staff: sales) · cancel rate · return requests (share of orders) · failed delivery share · fees (and % of net sales) · gross margin (owner only) · time to ship · time to deliver. Sort by net sales.
- Fees can be negative (charged by the marketplace); compare as absolute values. Gross margin = net sales − COGS; if COGS is 0 for a channel with sales, say cost prices are missing.
- Mark a channel when its cancel rate is at least 1.5× the shop average, or its fees exceed 20% of its net sales, or its time to deliver is at least 1.5× the fastest channel.
- Next: `/omisell:fees-by-channel` to see which fee type drives a high fee, `/omisell:courier-scorecard` for slow delivery, `/omisell:anomaly-check` when a channel's cancels jumped.

## Do not

- Do not rank channels on sales alone when cancels, returns or fees change the picture.
- Do not mix currencies: a business sells in one country, but if rows carry different currencies, show them separately.
